# 0.12: real space

## Goal
Space looks the way it does because of the objects in it. Nebulae, star clusters, star remnants, black holes, asteroid belts and comets are all generated by physical rules from the seed. Each one is a place you can fly to. Nothing on screen is decoration, and the game stays inside its frame budgets.

## Why
- The sky has a noise band and round galaxy blobs (`src/render/sky.wesl:52`, `src/render/starfield/specks.rs:58`). 0.11 step 14b replaces them with real galaxy light.
- Space holds only stars, planets, moons and rings. There is no gas, no small bodies and no dead stars. Real space is full of them, and each one comes from rules the engine already has: star mass and class (`src/universe/star.rs`), galaxy density by region (`src/universe/density.rs`) and Kepler orbits (`src/universe/orbit.rs`).

## Owner rules
- Pretty, realistic and fast.
- What you see is real: every pixel of light comes from an object that exists in the universe, at the place it appears.
- No cosmetics.
- If the owner asks for too much, say so, with frame-time numbers.

## Assumptions
- It builds on 0.11:
  - real-size planets, stars at real radii and orbits in AU (`PLANETS.md` step 1);
  - the galaxy light map with its dust term (`PLANETS.md` step 14b);
  - moons by formation (`PLANETS.md` step 2).
- No network protocol change. Every object is a pure function of seed, place and universe time, so every player sees the same sky and the same comet.
- The bodies the robot can land on (large asteroids, comet nuclei) reuse the globe. Small bodies are already supported down to 3 km.

## Approach
Each object is a pure generator in `src/universe/`, like stars and moons. It gets a level of detail in three tiers:

1. **Far.** It goes into the galaxy light map as emission and absorption. The map is baked off the main thread and costs nothing per frame beyond one texture lookup.
2. **Middle.** A ray-march with few steps for volumes, instanced points for rocks, or a lensing pass for black holes.
3. **Near.** Full detail, for at most the one or two objects you are inside or beside.

Volumes render at a quarter resolution into a buffer the pixelate pass upscales. This copies the pattern of the clouds (`src/render/clouds/`), with 1/16 of the pixels.

The alternative is a separate skybox of nebula pictures. It is rejected because it is decoration: you could not fly into it, and its light would not light anything.

## Steps

### Phase S1: rules (pure, parallel)
1. `src/universe/life_cycle.rs` (new): star life stages.
   - Initial mass from a Kroupa initial mass function. Age from the galaxy region in `density.rs`: old in the core and halo, young in the arms.
   - Stage from mass and age: main sequence, giant, white dwarf (initial mass under about 8 solar masses), neutron star (about 8 to 25), black hole (above about 25).
   - The star records of `star.rs` get their stage, so giants are drawn large and red, and remnants small.

   Verify:
   - A test over 100,000 stars checks that the shares of each stage match an IMF with the region's ages, within ±10 percent.
   - The home star is a main-sequence G star.
   - The same seed gives the same stages.
2. `src/universe/events.rs` (new): supernovae and pulsars in time.
   - A massive star's death time is its birth plus its computed lifetime.
   - It explodes when the universe clock passes that time. It brightens for weeks of game time, then leaves a remnant shell expanding at about 10,000 km/s.
   - A young neutron star is a pulsar with a real spin period (ms to s) and a beam axis. Its beam is seen only when it crosses the camera.

   Verify:
   - A test that the supernova rate in a home-like galaxy is a few per century of universe time.
   - A test that a remnant's radius grows with its age.
   - A test that a pulsar's flashes match its period.
3. `src/universe/nebula.rs` (new): gas, all from the arm and dust terms of `density.rs`.
   - Molecular clouds and dark nebulae.
   - Emission (H II) regions around young O and B stars. Their ionised radius comes from the star's ionising output (the Strömgren radius), and their colour from the Hα, OIII and Hβ lines.
   - Reflection nebulae: dust near bright B stars, blue from scattered starlight.
   - Planetary nebulae around white dwarfs younger than about 20,000 years, mostly OIII teal.
   - Supernova remnants from step 2.

   Verify:
   - A test that every emission region has an O or B star inside its radius.
   - A test that clouds lie along the real arms (more than 80 percent within an arm's width).
   - A test that colours follow the line ratios.
4. `src/universe/clusters.rs` (new): open clusters (young and blue) along the arms, globular clusters (old and gold) in the halo. Their stars join the real star list near the camera. Verify: a test that cluster stars come out of `stars_near` with the cluster's age and that globular clusters lie in the halo.
5. `src/universe/belts.rs` (new): small bodies in each system.
   - An asteroid belt between the snow line (about 2.7 AU × √luminosity) and the first giant, with Kirkwood gaps at that giant's 3:1, 5:2 and 2:1 resonances.
   - Trojans at the giant's L4 and L5 points.
   - A Kuiper belt beyond the outermost giant.
   - Each rock is a Kepler orbit drawn from `hash(system, cell, slot)`. Rocks are reached by orbital cell, never listed whole. The largest are landable bodies.

   Verify:
   - A test that no rock lies inside a gap (more than 95 percent outside).
   - A test that the rock positions at time t are the same for two callers.
   - A test that rocks within 5,000 km of a point are found in under 0.3 ms.
6. `src/universe/comets.rs` (new): a few comets per system on eccentric Kepler orbits from the Oort cloud.
   - Activity starts within about 3 AU of the star, from sublimation.
   - A straight blue ion tail points away from the star, and a curved yellow dust tail lies along the orbit.

   Verify:
   - A test that the tails' directions follow from the star direction and the orbital velocity.
   - A test that a comet has no tail beyond 4 AU.
   - A test that orbits are periodic.
7. `src/universe/holes.rs` (new): black holes.
   - A supermassive black hole sits in the central void of every galaxy, with a mass from the galaxy's size.
   - Stellar black holes come from step 1.
   - An accretion disc exists only when something feeds it: a close companion star, or an active galaxy core.

   Verify: a test that a disc exists exactly when a feeder exists, and that the disc's inner edge is at the innermost stable orbit (3 Schwarzschild radii for a non-rotating hole).

### Phase S2: drawing (parallel, after S1 merges)
8. `src/render/galaxy_light.rs` (from 0.11 step 14b): add the emission and absorption of nebulae, remnants and clusters to the far bake. Verify: smoke screenshots from inside an arm show red and teal patches where the real H II regions are, and dark lanes where the clouds are. The bake stays under 400 ms off the main thread.
9. `src/render/nebula/` (new): a ray-march of the nebula within reach. It uses 24 to 48 steps at a quarter resolution, jittered and blended over time, with emission and absorption from the same density as step 3. Verify: a GPU timing in the smoke run stays under 0.6 ms at 1080p inside a nebula, and the far bake and the ray-march agree in brightness within 10 percent at the hand-over distance.
10. `src/render/belts.rs` (new): instanced low-poly rocks for the orbital cells within reach (one mesh per rock shape, about 8 shapes), a faint dust band for the rest of the belt, and the same rocks as solids near the ship. Verify: under 0.3 ms CPU and one draw call per shape with 20,000 visible rocks; the ship test cannot fly through a rock.
11. `src/render/comets.rs` (new): the coma and both tails as camera-facing ribbons along their analytic curves. Their light also goes into the galaxy light map when the comet is far. Verify: under 0.1 ms per comet; a screenshot near the star shows both tails.
12. `src/render/holes.rs` (new): gravitational lensing of the sky map with Bruneton's precomputed real-time method, only within a few hundred hole radii. The accretion disc is drawn with its colour and its brighter near side from its orbital speed. Verify: under 0.3 ms at 1080p when active; a test that the photon ring sits at about 2.6 hole radii on screen.
13. `src/render/starfield/specks.rs`: draw giants, dwarfs and pulsars by stage (size, colour, and the beam flash). Verify: the starfield tests pass and a pulsar test flashes at its period.

### Phase S3: travel and the computer
14. `src/computer/` and `src/ship/route/`: nebulae, clusters, belts, comets and black holes become targets on the board computer and the map, with real names and details. The ship's drop-out distance from a black hole comes from its tidal force. Verify: computer tests list them; a route to a comet drops out within 1,000 km of it.

### Phase S4: ship 0.12.0
15. Smoke screenshots of a nebula from far, near and inside, a belt, a comet near its star, a pulsar, and the black hole at the home galaxy's centre, each with frame-time numbers. Then `mise run check`, the changelog and the release. Verify: every number is inside its budget, and the `release-preflight` flow is green.

## Performance budgets
| Part | Budget |
| --- | --- |
| Volumes (nebula ray-march) | 0.6 ms GPU at 1080p, only near one nebula |
| Rocks | 0.3 ms CPU, instanced |
| Comets | 0.1 ms each, at most 3 active |
| Lensing | 0.3 ms GPU, only near a hole |
| All of space together | 1 ms GPU and 0.5 ms CPU in the worst view |
| Far bake | 400 ms off the main thread, never blocking |

If a feature misses its budget, the merger reports it to the owner with numbers and the options (cut, defer or simplify) before it ships.

## Boundaries
- Ask first:
  - A new crate. None is planned.
  - A second ray-marched volume on screen at once.
  - Binary star systems. Half of real stars are binaries, but that reworks orbits and systems, so it is a release of its own.
- Never:
  - A texture or mesh of something that does not exist in the universe at that place.
  - Light without a source.
  - A cost raised past its budget without the owner's yes.

## Done when
- From a planet's night side, the galaxy band shows real red and teal nebulae and dark lanes where they really are.
- Flying into a nebula, it turns into a volume around you.
- Belts have gaps where the physics says.
- A comet near its star grows two tails pointing the right ways.
- Pulsars flash.
- The black hole at the galaxy's centre bends the stars behind it.
- Every frame number is inside its budget, and `mise run check` is green.

## Risks
- Volume cost on weak GPUs. The quarter resolution and step count are settings with a floor; below the floor, the nebula falls back to the far bake, which is still real.
- Lensing correctness: Bruneton's method covers non-rotating holes. Rotating (Kerr) holes are approximated as non-rotating.
- Scale: the real-scale universe of 0.11 makes belts sparse. Rocks are far apart in a real belt, so the belt reads as a dust band more than a field of boulders. That is the real look and the plan keeps it.

## Skip
- Binary and multiple star systems (see Ask first).
- Rotating black holes, exactly.
- Interstellar travel at real light-year distances: the warp stays.
